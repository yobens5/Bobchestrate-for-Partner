 PART 1 — BUSINESS SPECIFICATION
================================================================================

1.1  PURPOSE & BACKGROUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SuperChampion is a (fictional) retail supermarket chain operating across Israel.
This data warehouse consolidates the chain's commercial activity — sales,
inventory, workforce and revenue — into an analytics-ready, dimensional model
designed to power Business Intelligence (BI), dashboards, and natural-language
"text-to-data" agents.

The warehouse is intentionally a DATA-WAREHOUSE model (dimensional / star schema
with pre-aggregated marts), NOT a transactional ERP/OLTP schema. Tables are
"pre-chewed" so that BI tools and AI agents can query them directly with simple
joins and aggregations.

1.2  SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IN SCOPE
  * Point-of-sale results by store, product and day (sales & revenue)
  * Inventory / stock position by store and product (weekly snapshots)
  * Workforce scheduling & labor cost (employee shifts)
  * Pre-aggregated BI marts (daily, monthly, store, category, chain level)

OUT OF SCOPE
  * Operational transaction processing (no live order/checkout system)
  * Customer-level / loyalty / basket-line detail
  * Procurement, finance ledger, HR master beyond shift costing
  * Real-time streaming (data is batch-generated)

1.3  SUBJECT AREAS (BUSINESS DOMAINS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A. SALES & REVENUE   — units, gross/net revenue, discounts, cost, margin
  B. INVENTORY / STOCK — units on hand, reorder status, stock value, cover days
  C. WORKFORCE         — shifts, scheduled vs actual hours, labor cost, overtime
  D. PROFITABILITY     — store P&L: margin minus labor = contribution

1.4  INTENDED USERS & CONSUMPTION CHANNELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Retail/BI analysts — dashboards (revenue, margin, labor, inventory health)
  * Store & regional managers — store performance and P&L
  * Supply / merchandising — category performance, stock-out risk
  * AI text-to-data agent — IBM watsonx Orchestrate agent generated by the
    "Text2Data Agent Builder"; the agent queries the MART schema in Db2.
  * BI tools — Metabase, Power BI, Tableau, DBeaver, etc. (standard SQL).

1.5  GEOGRAPHY, CURRENCY & TIME COVERAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Geography  : Israel — 24 stores across the 6 administrative districts
               (Tel Aviv, Jerusalem, Haifa, Central, Northern, Southern).
  Currency   : Israeli New Shekel (ILS, ₪). All monetary values are in ILS.
  Time span  : 2024-01-01 → 2025-12-31 (731 days, 2 full years for YoY).
  Calendar   : Israeli working week — Sunday–Thursday are work days; the
               weekend is FRIDAY + SATURDAY.

1.6  BUSINESS RULES & DOMAIN SEMANTICS (ISRAELI RETAIL)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These rules are encoded in the data so analyses reflect real Israeli retail
behaviour:

  R1  WEEKEND DEFINITION
      The weekend is Friday + Saturday (dim_date.is_weekend = true).

  R2  SHABBAT TRADING
      Most stores are CLOSED on Saturday (Shabbat) → no sales rows.
      Only stores flagged is_shabbat_open trade on Saturday, at ~45% of a
      normal day. Shabbat-open cities: Tel Aviv, Haifa, Eilat, Tiberias.

  R3  FRIDAY PEAK
      Friday is the pre-Shabbat shopping peak (~1.7x a normal weekday).
      Sunday (start of week) runs slightly high (~1.15x).

  R4  HOLIDAY CLOSURES
      On Jewish public holidays (Pesach, Rosh Hashana, Yom Kippur, Sukkot,
      Shavuot, Independence Day, Purim, Hanukkah) stores are closed → no
      sales. The day BEFORE a holiday (holiday eve) is a major peak (~1.9x).
      NOTE: holiday dates are well-known approximate Gregorian dates for demo.

  R5  STORE FORMAT SCALES VOLUME
      Hypermarket ~2.3x, Supermarket ~1.0x, Express ~0.45x baseline volume.

  R6  PRICING & MARGIN
      unit_cost is ~58–75% of unit_price (gross margin ~25–42% by item).
      Line discounts range 0–12% of gross revenue.

  R7  PRODUCT ATTRIBUTES
      Each SKU carries a kosher flag (is_kosher) — relevant in the Israeli
      market. Food items are kosher; most non-food (household, beauty) are not.

  R8  WORKFORCE PAY
      Hourly rates reflect Israeli norms: cashiers/stockers ~₪34–40/h,
      shift managers ~₪52/h, store managers ~₪80/h, with specialist roles
      (butcher/baker/security/cleaner) in between.

  R9  SHIFT SCHEDULING
      Shifts run Sunday–Friday (no Saturday, no holidays). Friday is a short
      day (5h). Full-time staff work ~82% of eligible days, part-time ~50%,
      students ~38%. Overtime is flagged when actual > scheduled hours.

  R10 INVENTORY SNAPSHOTS
      Stock is captured WEEKLY on Sundays (start of the Israeli week).
      Reorder point = ~2x daily sell-through; an item is "below reorder"
      when on-hand < reorder point (~18–27% of SKUs at any snapshot).

1.7  METRIC & KPI GLOSSARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  gross_revenue        = units_sold * unit_price
  discount_amount      = promotional reduction applied to gross
  net_revenue          = gross_revenue - discount_amount      (the headline "revenue")
  cost_amount          = units_sold * unit_cost               (cost of goods sold)
  gross_margin         = net_revenue - cost_amount
  margin_pct           = gross_margin / net_revenue * 100
  num_transactions     = number of baskets containing the product
  avg_basket_value     = net_revenue / num_transactions
  labor_cost           = actual_hours * hourly_rate
  contribution         = gross_margin - labor_cost            (store-level profit proxy)
  labor_pct_of_revenue = labor_cost / net_revenue * 100
  units_on_hand        = stock units physically in store
  reorder_point        = threshold below which replenishment is triggered
  stock_value          = units_on_hand * unit_cost
  days_of_supply       = units_on_hand / average daily sell-through

1.8  REPRESENTATIVE BUSINESS QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * What is net revenue by store / district / month? Top and bottom performers?
  * How strong is the Friday peak vs the rest of the week? Which stores trade
    on Shabbat and how much do they make?
  * What is each store's monthly P&L (margin minus labor = contribution)?
  * Which product categories drive revenue and which carry the best margin?
  * Where is labor cost too high as a % of revenue?
  * Which stores have the most SKUs below reorder (stock-out risk)?
  * What is total chain revenue, margin and average basket per month? YoY trend?

1.9  DATA-QUALITY NOTES & CAVEATS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * SYNTHETIC DATA. All stores, products, employees and figures are generated
    for demonstration; they do not represent a real company.
  * Fact tables are randomly generated; exact row counts vary slightly on each
    rebuild. Marts are deterministic aggregations of whatever facts exist.
  * Sales facts are SPARSE (not every product sells in every store every day).
  * Holiday dates are approximate Gregorian equivalents.
  * Referential integrity is guaranteed by the build (keys always resolve), but
    foreign-key CONSTRAINTS are intentionally omitted in the Db2 copy to keep
    bulk loading order-independent.

 PART 2 — TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION

2.1  ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Dimensional (Kimball-style) model:

  * DIMENSIONS  — descriptive context (who/what/where/when): date, store,
                  product, employee.
  * FACTS       — measurable events at a defined grain: sales, inventory
                  snapshots, employee shifts.
  * MARTS       — pre-aggregated, denormalised tables built FROM the facts for
                  direct BI/agent consumption (the "pre-chewed" layer).

Data flow:
  dimensions + facts  ──aggregate──▶  marts  ──consume──▶  BI tools / AI agent

2.2  PHYSICAL DEPLOYMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SOURCE (build/dev) : PostgreSQL 17 (local, Homebrew) — database "superchampion"
  TARGET (cloud)     : IBM Db2 on Cloud (SaaS), database "bludb" — Db2 12.01

The model is identical in both; the Db2 copy is produced by exporting the
Postgres tables to CSV and bulk-loading them. The Db2 copy is the one consumed
by the watsonx Orchestrate text-to-data agent.

2.3  SCHEMA / NAMESPACE LAYOUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Schema "dwh"  (Db2: DWH)   — dimensions + facts
  Schema "mart" (Db2: MART)  — pre-aggregated BI tables

  PostgreSQL default search_path = mart, dwh, public
  (unqualified table names resolve to the marts first).

2.4  NAMING & DATA-TYPE CONVENTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * snake_case for all schema, table and column names.
  * Dimensions: dim_<entity>;  facts: fact_<event>;  surrogate key: <entity>_key.
  * date_key is an integer in YYYYMMDD form (e.g. 20240101).
  * Money: PostgreSQL NUMERIC(p,2)  →  Db2 DECIMAL(p,2)  (ILS).
  * Booleans: PostgreSQL BOOLEAN    →  Db2 SMALLINT (0/1).
  * Text:    PostgreSQL TEXT        →  Db2 VARCHAR(n).
  * Db2 folds unquoted identifiers to UPPERCASE (tables appear as DWH.*, MART.*).
  * Two columns renamed in Db2 to avoid the reserved word DATE:
        dim_date.date              → DWH.DIM_DATE.cal_date
        daily_store_sales.date     → MART.DAILY_STORE_SALES.sales_date

2.5  DIMENSION TABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  dwh.dim_date            Grain: one row per calendar day
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
    date_key         INTEGER   PK   YYYYMMDD
    date / cal_date  DATE           calendar date (Db2: cal_date)
    day              INTEGER        day of month (1-31)
    month            INTEGER        month number (1-12)
    year             INTEGER        year
    quarter          INTEGER        quarter (1-4)
    day_of_week      INTEGER        0=Sun .. 6=Sat (Postgres dow)
    iso_dow          INTEGER        1=Mon .. 7=Sun
    day_name         VARCHAR(16)    e.g. "Friday"
    month_name       VARCHAR(16)    e.g. "April"
    week_of_year     INTEGER        ISO week number
    is_weekend       BOOL/SMALLINT  true for Friday & Saturday
    is_holiday       BOOL/SMALLINT  Jewish public holiday
    holiday_name     VARCHAR(64)    holiday label (nullable)
    is_holiday_eve   BOOL/SMALLINT  day before a holiday

  dwh.dim_store           Grain: one row per store
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
    store_key        INTEGER   PK   surrogate key
    store_code       VARCHAR(16)    business code, e.g. "SC-TLV-01"
    store_name       VARCHAR(64)    e.g. "SuperChampion Dizengoff"
    city             VARCHAR(48)    Israeli city
    district         VARCHAR(24)    Tel Aviv|Jerusalem|Haifa|Central|Northern|Southern
    store_type       VARCHAR(16)    Hypermarket | Supermarket | Express
    size_sqm         INTEGER        floor area (m2)
    opening_date     DATE           store opening date
    is_shabbat_open  BOOL/SMALLINT  trades on Saturday
    latitude         DECIMAL(9,6)   geo (nullable)
    longitude        DECIMAL(9,6)   geo (nullable)
    is_active        BOOL/SMALLINT  currently operating

  dwh.dim_product         Grain: one row per SKU
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
    product_key      INTEGER   PK   surrogate key
    sku              VARCHAR(16)    stock-keeping unit code
    product_name     VARCHAR(64)    e.g. "Milk 3% 1L"
    category         VARCHAR(32)    Dairy|Bakery|Produce|Meat & Fish|Frozen|
                                    Beverages|Snacks|Household|Health & Beauty|
                                    Wine & Spirits|Pantry
    subcategory      VARCHAR(32)    finer grouping
    brand            VARCHAR(48)    Israeli brand (Tnuva, Osem, Elite, ...)
    unit_cost        DECIMAL(10,2)  cost per unit (ILS)
    unit_price       DECIMAL(10,2)  retail price per unit (ILS)
    base_daily_units DECIMAL(8,2)   popularity/velocity seed
    is_kosher        BOOL/SMALLINT  kosher flag
    supplier_name    VARCHAR(48)    supplier

  dwh.dim_employee        Grain: one row per employee
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
    employee_key     INTEGER   PK   surrogate key
    employee_code    VARCHAR(16)    e.g. "EMP-00042"
    full_name        VARCHAR(64)    employee name
    store_key        INTEGER        home store (-> dim_store)
    role             VARCHAR(24)    Cashier|Stocker|Shift Manager|Store Manager|
                                    Butcher|Baker|Security|Cleaner
    employment_type  VARCHAR(16)    Full-time | Part-time | Student
    hire_date        DATE           hire date
    hourly_rate      DECIMAL(8,2)   pay rate (ILS/hour)
    is_active        BOOL/SMALLINT  currently employed

2.6  FACT TABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  dwh.fact_sales          Grain: store x product x day (rows only where sold)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
    sale_key         BIGINT    PK
    date_key         INTEGER   -> dim_date
    store_key        INTEGER   -> dim_store
    product_key      INTEGER   -> dim_product
    units_sold       INTEGER        units sold that day
    gross_revenue    DECIMAL(12,2)  units * unit_price
    discount_amount  DECIMAL(12,2)  promo discount
    net_revenue      DECIMAL(12,2)  gross - discount
    cost_amount      DECIMAL(12,2)  units * unit_cost
    gross_margin     DECIMAL(12,2)  net_revenue - cost_amount
    num_transactions INTEGER        baskets containing this product

  dwh.fact_inventory_snapshot   Grain: store x product x week (Sunday snapshot)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
    snapshot_key     BIGINT    PK
    date_key         INTEGER   -> dim_date (a Sunday)
    store_key        INTEGER   -> dim_store
    product_key      INTEGER   -> dim_product
    units_on_hand    INTEGER        physical stock
    units_on_order   INTEGER        replenishment in transit
    reorder_point    INTEGER        replenishment threshold
    is_below_reorder BOOL/SMALLINT  on_hand < reorder_point
    stock_value      DECIMAL(12,2)  units_on_hand * unit_cost
    days_of_supply   DECIMAL(6,1)   on_hand / daily sell-through

  dwh.fact_employee_shift  Grain: one row per worked shift (employee x day)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
    shift_key        BIGINT    PK
    date_key         INTEGER   -> dim_date (Sun-Fri, non-holiday)
    store_key        INTEGER   -> dim_store
    employee_key     INTEGER   -> dim_employee
    shift_type       VARCHAR(12)    Morning | Afternoon | Night
    scheduled_hours  DECIMAL(4,1)   planned hours
    actual_hours     DECIMAL(4,1)   worked hours
    labor_cost       DECIMAL(10,2)  actual_hours * hourly_rate
    is_overtime      BOOL/SMALLINT  actual > scheduled

2.7  BI MART TABLES (pre-aggregated)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  mart.daily_store_sales        Grain: store x day.  PK (date_key, store_key)
      Derived from fact_sales (+ dim_date, dim_store).
      Cols: date/sales_date, date_key, store_key, store_name, city, district,
            store_type, units_sold, gross_revenue, discount_amount, net_revenue,
            total_cost, gross_margin, margin_pct, num_transactions,
            distinct_products, avg_basket_value.

  mart.monthly_store_pnl        Grain: store x month.  PK (year, month, store_key)
      Sales aggregated + labor cost joined; contribution = margin - labor.
      Cols: year, month, month_name, store_key, store_name, city, district,
            net_revenue, gross_margin, labor_cost, labor_pct_of_revenue,
            contribution, num_transactions.

  mart.category_monthly_sales   Grain: category x month.  PK (year, month, category)
      Cols: year, month, category, units_sold, net_revenue, gross_margin,
            margin_pct.

  mart.store_labor_monthly      Grain: store x month.  PK (year, month, store_key)
      From fact_employee_shift.
      Cols: year, month, store_key, store_name, district, total_hours,
            overtime_hours, labor_cost, headcount, avg_hourly_cost.

  mart.inventory_health_current Grain: store (latest snapshot).  PK (store_key)
      Cols: store_key, store_name, district, snapshot_date, skus_stocked,
            total_stock_value, skus_below_reorder, pct_below_reorder,
            avg_days_of_supply.

  mart.monthly_chain_kpi        Grain: month (whole chain).  PK (year, month)
      Cols: year, month, month_name, active_stores, net_revenue, gross_margin,
            margin_pct, labor_cost, contribution, num_transactions,
            avg_basket_value.

2.8  STAR-SCHEMA RELATIONSHIPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                         dwh.dim_date
                              |
                              | date_key
                              v
  dim_store --store_key--> fact_sales <--product_key-- dim_product
                              ^
                              | (also referenced by:)
        fact_inventory_snapshot  (date_key, store_key, product_key)
        fact_employee_shift      (date_key, store_key, employee_key)

  dim_employee --employee_key--> fact_employee_shift
  dim_store    --store_key------> dim_employee (home store)

  Marts denormalise the above into single wide tables (store_name, district,
  category, etc. carried inline) so no joins are required for common BI.

  NOTE: relationships are logical. In PostgreSQL the keys are clean and indexed;
  in Db2 FK constraints are intentionally not created (load simplicity).

2.10 INDEXES (PostgreSQL source)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Primary keys on every table (surrogate or composite, see above).
  Secondary indexes on fact foreign keys:
    fact_sales(date_key), (store_key), (product_key)
    fact_inventory_snapshot(date_key), (store_key)
    fact_employee_shift(date_key), (store_key), (employee_key)
  Marts carry their natural-key primary keys (sufficient for BI scans).

2.11 BUILD / ETL PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SQL build (PostgreSQL), run in order by scripts/setup.sh:
    sql/00_schema.sql      DDL: schemas, dimensions, facts, marts (empty)
    sql/10_dimensions.sql  populate dim_date, dim_store, dim_product, dim_employee
    sql/20_facts.sql       generate fact_sales, inventory, shifts (+ indexes, ANALYZE)
    sql/30_marts.sql       build the 6 marts via INSERT ... SELECT aggregations
    sql/99_verify.sql      row counts + sample BI checks

  Export & cloud load:
    scripts/export_for_db2.sh   PostgreSQL -> CSV (db2_export/csv/)
    db2_export/ddl/superchampion_db2_ddl.sql   Db2 DDL (translated types)
    scripts/load_to_db2.py      create Db2 schemas/tables + bulk-load CSVs (SSL)

2.12 POSTGRESQL → DB2 MIGRATION MAPPING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Type mapping:
    TEXT             -> VARCHAR(n)         (sized per column)
    BOOLEAN          -> SMALLINT (0/1)     (CSV exports booleans as 0/1)
    SERIAL/BIGSERIAL -> INTEGER / BIGINT   (key values carried from source)
    NUMERIC(p,s)     -> DECIMAL(p,s)
    DATE             -> DATE
  Identifier handling:
    unquoted names fold to UPPERCASE in Db2 (DWH.*, MART.*).
  Renamed columns (reserved word DATE):
    dim_date.date          -> cal_date
    daily_store_sales.date -> sales_date
  Constraints:
    primary keys kept; foreign keys omitted (order-independent loading).
  CSV format:
    UTF-8, comma-delimited, header row, ISO dates (YYYY-MM-DD), NULL = empty.

2.14 REFRESH / RELOAD PROCEDURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Rebuild PostgreSQL from scratch:
    ./scripts/setup.sh            (or ./scripts/reset.sh to drop & recreate DB)
  Re-export to CSV:
    ./scripts/export_for_db2.sh
  Reload into Db2 (idempotent: drops & recreates tables, then loads):
    .venv/bin/python scripts/load_to_db2.py
  Cadence: on-demand (synthetic). Inventory snapshots are weekly (Sundays);
  marts are full-refresh rebuilds (TRUNCATE + INSERT) — no incremental logic.

3.1  SAMPLE ANALYTICAL QUERIES (PostgreSQL syntax; Db2 use UPPER names + cal_date/sales_date)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  -- Net revenue by day of week (Friday peaks, Saturday low)
  SELECT d.day_name, SUM(s.net_revenue) AS revenue_ils
  FROM mart.daily_store_sales s
  JOIN dwh.dim_date d ON d.date = s.date
  GROUP BY d.day_name, d.iso_dow
  ORDER BY d.iso_dow;

  -- Top stores by 2025 revenue
  SELECT store_name, district, SUM(net_revenue) AS revenue_ils
  FROM mart.daily_store_sales
  WHERE EXTRACT(YEAR FROM date) = 2025
  GROUP BY store_name, district
  ORDER BY revenue_ils DESC
  LIMIT 10;

  -- Store P&L (contribution) for a month
  SELECT store_name, net_revenue, gross_margin, labor_cost,
         labor_pct_of_revenue, contribution
  FROM mart.monthly_store_pnl
  WHERE year = 2025 AND month = 12
  ORDER BY contribution DESC;

  -- Category margins
  SELECT category, SUM(net_revenue) AS revenue_ils,
         ROUND(AVG(margin_pct),1) AS margin_pct
  FROM mart.category_monthly_sales
  GROUP BY category
  ORDER BY revenue_ils DESC;

  -- Inventory stock-out risk
  SELECT store_name, district, skus_below_reorder, pct_below_reorder,
         total_stock_value
  FROM mart.inventory_health_current
  ORDER BY pct_below_reorder DESC;
